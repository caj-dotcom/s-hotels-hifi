# S Hotels Jamaica — Core Research Summary

One-page references for the parent brand and both hotels, plus the structural notes from the research board.

## S Hotels Jamaica (Parent)

### What it is
S Hotels Jamaica is the parent brand for two boutique hotels built by Christopher Issa. It began with the conversion of the Spanish Court Shopping Centre into Spanish Court Hotel in Kingston (2009) and later expanded with the transformation of the former Breezes property into S Hotel Montego Bay (2019). The brand name was formalised in 2024. Both properties sit under one name and one website. They share Jamaican culture, service standards, and the S logo, but keep distinct experiences. Montego Bay is the beach property on Doctor’s Cave. Kingston is the urban property in the city. The parent site introduces the lineage and lets guests switch to either hotel.

### Brand position
Approachable, premium, and classy. Both hotels sell a Jamaican experience rather than a generic resort or business hotel. The sites must convert with imagery that shows atmosphere, culture, and the stay itself. Inclusive packages remain available but do not define the brand. Significant heritage features (Bob Marley statues, record players, artwork) deserve dedicated space so guests who book for one highlight can find it easily. Colour direction moves away from strong red. Font and palette can be chosen by the team. Logo stays.

## S Hotel Montego Bay

### What it is
Contemporary boutique all-inclusive on Jimmy Cliff Boulevard with direct access to Doctor’s Cave Beach. 117–120 rooms. Urban sophistication with a laid-back resort feel. Life-size Jamaican icons and artwork throughout. Bob Marley record players in rooms. Sky Deck, Irie Spa, and multiple restaurants.

### Ideal guest profiles
- Couples seeking a relaxed boutique all-inclusive with Doctor’s Cave Beach, art, and Jamaican character rather than large-resort entertainment.
- Honeymooners who want a calm, design-forward stay.
- Relaxed families with older children (8+) who prefer the beach and culture over constant activities.
- Solo travellers looking for personal service and a place that feels like a museum you can stay in.
- Guests who value the intimate size and return for the atmosphere.

### Not for
- Party tourists
- Guests looking for a large resort
- People expecting constant entertainment
- Families with very young children who need full kids facilities

### Brand position
Lead with Doctor’s Cave Beach and the cultural elements already inside the property. Show the art, the calm, the personal service, and the slower pace so the right guests recognise it as a museum-like Jamaican experience they can stay in, not another all-inclusive.

## S Hotel Kingston

### What it is
Boutique hotel in the heart of Kingston for business and leisure. 123 rooms. Minimalist design, Roman tubs, rooftop pool. Strong banqueting. Artwork in common areas. Close to the Bob Marley Museum and Devon House. Noted Jamaican breakfast.

### Ideal guest profiles
- Business travellers who need a calm, professional base in New Kingston with meeting space and reliable service.
- Jamaican diaspora and returning visitors who want a familiar, warm urban hotel close to the city’s culture.
- Local guests looking for weekend hangouts and relaxation.
- Guests who value design, Jamaican culture, and location near key city sites.
- Couples or short-stay visitors seeking a refined city hotel rather than a resort.

### Not for
- Guests looking for a large resort
- People expecting a party atmosphere
- Guests wanting constant entertainment
- People looking for a beach-focused stay

### Brand position
Sell the atmosphere of staying in the heartbeat of Kingston. Present it as the hotel that feels like Jamaica rather than a brand box. Emphasise the quiet professional base, the cultural proximity, and the service that business and returning guests already score highly.

## Structural notes

### Maximum section count per page
Parent sites stay between 4 and 5 main sections. Child sites run 10 to 12.

**Why parent pages stay short**  
They exist to introduce the brand and move the user toward a specific hotel. Extra sections turn the parent into a catalogue and slow the decision. The stronger sites either keep it under 5–6 clear blocks or group many small items under a few headings so the page still feels simple.

**Child / property pages**  
These can run longer (commonly 8–12 sections) because the guest has already chosen the hotel. Rooms, dining, wellness, experiences, location, and social proof all have a place. Beyond that the page starts to repeat itself or feels long on mobile.

**For S Hotels**  
Parent site: 4–6 main sections. Each hotel site: 8–12 sections, with the most important experiences (beach or city atmosphere, art, dining, spa) appearing early and the rest supporting them.

### The gap S Hotels can own
Most Jamaican hotel sites are weak. They list rooms, restaurants, and booking links without selling atmosphere, culture, or clear expectations. Guests arrive with the wrong idea of what the stay is, which shows up in the reviews.

The stronger global sites (Aman, Rosewood, Capella, Six Senses, Habitas) do a better job of leading with atmosphere and photography. They still leave space for S Hotels. None of them own a Jamaican cultural story. None of them present a hotel as a museum-like experience a guest can stay in. None of them set expectations early enough to filter the wrong guests before they book.

S Hotels can own three things the current Jamaican sites miss and the global sites do not claim:

- Jamaican culture and heritage shown through real art, music, and place, not generic resort language.
- The museum-like feeling already present in the properties (life-size icons, record players, artwork), treated as a reason to stay rather than a background detail.
- Clear expectation-setting on the homepage and in key sections so the right guests recognise the hotel and the wrong ones self-select out before they book.

This gap is open because the Jamaican sites are not doing it.
